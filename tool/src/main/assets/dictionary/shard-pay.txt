pay	PAY	v.t.; pret. and pp. paid	To discharge a debt; to deliver to a creditor the value of the debt, either in money or goods, to his acceptance or satisfaction, by which the obligation of the debtor is discharged.	To discharge a duty created by promise or by custom or by the moral law; as, to pay a debt of honor or of kindness. You have paid down More penitence, than done trespass.	To fulfill; to perform what is promised; as, to pay one's vows.	To render what is due to a superior, or demanded by civility or courtesy; as, to pay respect to a magistrate; to pay due honor to parents.	To beat. For which, or pay me quickly, or I'll pay you.	To reward; to recompense; as, to pay for kindness with neglect. To pay for, to make amends; to atone by suffering. Men often pay for their mistakes with loss of property or reputation, sometimes with life.	To give an equivalent for any thing purchased. To pay or pay over, in seamen's language, to daub or besmear the surface of any body, to preserve it from injury by water or weather. To pay the bottom of a vessel, to cover it with a composition of tallow, sulphur, rosin, etc.; to bream. To pay a mast or yard, to besmear it with tar, turpentine, rosin, tallow or varnish. PAY a seam, to pour melted pitch along it, so as to defend the oakum. To pay off; to make compensation to and discharge; as, to pay off the crew of a ship. To pay out, to slacken, extend or cause to run out; as, to pay out more cable.
pay	PAY	v.i.	To pay off, in seamen's language, is to fall to leeward, as the head of a ship. To pay on, to beat with vigor; to redouble blows. [Colloquial.]

pay-bill	PAY-BILL	n.	A bill of money to be paid to the soldiers of a company.
pay-day	PAY-DAY	n.	The day when payment is to be made or debts discharged; the day on which wages or money is stipulated to be paid.
pay-office	PAY-OFFICE	n.	A place or office where payment is made of public debts.
payable	PAYABLE	a.	That may or ought to be paid. In general, money is payable as soon as it is due, or at the time payment is stipulated, or at the expiration of the credit; but by the usage of merchants, three or more days of grace are allowed to the debtor, and a note due at the day when payment is promised, is not payable till the expiration of the days of grace.	That can be paid; that there is power to pay. Thanks are a tribute payable by the poorest.
