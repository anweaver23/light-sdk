sequel	SEQUEL	n.	That which follows; a succeeding part; as the sequel of a man's adventures or history.	Consequence; event. Lit the sun or moon cease, fail or swerve, and the sequel would be ruin.	Consequence inferred; consequentialness. [Little used.]
sequence	SEQUENCE	n.	A following, or that which follows; aconsequent.	Order of succession. How art thou king But by fair sequence and succession? Shak.	Series; arrangement; method.	In music, a regular alternate succession of similar chords.
sequent	SEQUENT	a.	Following; succeeding.	Consequential. [Little used.]
sequent	SEQUENT	n.	A follower. [Not in use.]
sequester	SEQUESTER	v.t.	To separate from the owner for a time; to seize or take possession of some property which belongs to another, and hold it tillthe profits hve paid the demand for which it is taken. Formerly the goods of a defendant in chancery, were, in the last resort, sequestered and detained to enforce the degrees of the court. and now the profits of a benefice are sequestered to pay the debts of the ecclesiastecs. Blackstone.	To take from parties in controversy and put into the possession of an indiffernt person.	To put aside; to remove; to separate; frome other things. I had wholly sequestered my civil affairs. Bacon.	To sequester one's self, to separate one's self from seciety; to withdraw or retire; to seclude one's self for the sake of privacy or solitude; as, to sequester one's self from action.	To cause to retire or withdraw into obscurity. It was his taylor and his cook, his fine fashions and his French ragouts which sequestered him. South.
sequester	SEQUESTER	v.i.	To decline, as a window, any concern with the estate of a husband.
sequestered	SEQUESTERED	pp.	Seized asnd detained for a time, to satisfy a demand; separated; also, being in retirement; secluded; private; as a sequestered situation.
sequestrable	SEQUESTRABLE	a.	That may be seqestered or separated; subject or liable to sequestration.
sequestrate	SEQUESTRATE	v.t.	To seqester. [It is less used than seqester, but exactly synonymous.]
sequestration	SEQUESTRATION	n.	The act of taking a thing from parties contending for it, and entrusting it to an indifferent person.	In the civil law, the act of the ordinary, disposing of the goods and chattels of one deceased, whose estate no will meddle with.	The act of taking property from the owner for a time, till the rents, issues and profits satisfy a demand.	The act of seizing the estate of a delinquent for the use of the state.	Separation; retirement; seclusion from society.	State of being separated or set aside.	Disunion; disjunction. [Not in use.]
sequestrator	SEQUESTRATOR	n.	One that sequesters property, or takes the possession of it for a time, to satilfy a demand out of its rents or profits.	One to whom the keeping of sequestered property is committed.
sequin	SEQUIN	n.	A gold coin of Venice and Turkey, of different value in different places. At Venice, its value is about 9s. 2d. sterling, or $2.04. In other parts of Italy, it is stated to be of the 9s. value, or $2. It is sometimes written chequin and zechin. [See Zechin.]
