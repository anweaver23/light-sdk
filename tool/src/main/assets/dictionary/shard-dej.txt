deject	DEJECT	v.t.	To cast down; usually, to cast down the countenance; to cause to fall with grief; to make to look sad or grieved, or to express discouragement. But gloomy were his eyes, dejected was his face.	To depress the spirits; to sink; to dispirit; to discourage; to dishearten. Nor think to die dejects my lofty mind.
deject	DEJECT	a.	Cast down; low-spirited.
dejected	DEJECTED	pp.	Cast down; depressed; grieved; discouraged.
dejectedly	DEJECTEDLY	adv.	In a dejected manner; sadly; heavily.
dejectedness	DEJECTEDNESS	n.	The state being cast down; lowness of spirits.
dejecting	DEJECTING	ppr.	Casting down; depressing; dispiriting.
dejection	DEJECTION	n.	A casting down; depression of mind; melancholy; lowness of spirits, occasioned by grief or misfortune.	Weakness; as dejection of appetite.	The act of voiding the excrements; or the matter ejected.
dejectly	DEJECTLY	adv.	In a downcast manner.
dejectory	DEJECTORY	a.	Having power or tending to cast down, or to promote evacuations by stool.
dejecture	DEJECTURE	n.	That which is ejected; excrements.
