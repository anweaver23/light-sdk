excuse	EXCUSE	v.t.	s as z. [Latin excuso; ex and causor, to blame. See Cause.]	To pardon; to free from the imputation of fault or blame; to acquit of guilt. We excuse a person in our own minds, when we acquit him of guilt or blame; or we excuse him by a declaration of that acquital.	To pardon, as a fault; to forgive entirely, or to admit to be little censurable, and to overlook. We excuse a fault, which admits of apology or extenuation; and we excuse irregular conduct, when extraordinary circumstances appear to justify it.	To free from an obligation or duty. I pray thee have me excused. Luke 14:18.	To remit; not to exact; as, to excuse a forfeiture.	To pardon; to admit an apology for. Excuse some courtly strains.	To throw off an imputation by apology. Think you that we excuse ourselves to you? 2 Corinthians 12:19.	To justify; to vindicate. Their thoughts accusing or else excusing one another. Romans 2:1.
excuse	EXCUSE	n.	A plea offered in extenuation of a fault or irregular deportment; apology. Every man has an excuse to offer for his neglect of duty; the debtor makes excuses for delay of payment.	The act of excusing or apologizing.	That which excuses; that which extenuates or justifies a fault. His inability to comply with the request must be his excuse
excuseless	EXCUSELESS	a.	Having no excuse; that for which no excuse or apology can be offered. [Little used.]
excuser	EXCUSER	n.	s as z. One who offers excuses or pleads for another.	One who excuses or forgives another.
excusing	EXCUSING	ppr.	s as z. Acquitting of guilt or fault; forgiving; overlooking.
excuss	EXCUSS	v.t.	To shake off; also, to seize and detain by law. [Not used.]
excussion	EXCUSSION	n.	A seizing by law. [Not used.]
