baza	BAZA	n.	A long, fine spun cotton from Jerusalem, whence it is called Jerusalem cotton.
bazar	BAZAR	n.	Among the Turks and Persians, an exchange, market-place, or place where goods are exposed to sale. Some bazars are open, others are covered with lofty ceilings or domes, pierced to give light. The bazar at Tauris will contain 30, 000 men.
bazat	BAZAT		A fortification or outer defense to a city or castle, consisting of an elevation of earth about three feet high, along the foot of the rampart.	A fortress at the end of a bridge, or at the outlet of a city, having a double wall with towers.	An opening in the wall of a fortress through which guns are leveled and fired upon an enemy. The French use the word also for an aperture in a wall to let in or drain off water; and the Spaniards, for a low wall round a church yard.
