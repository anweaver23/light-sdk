try	TRY	v.i.	To exert strength; to endeavor; to make an effort; to attempt. try to learn; try to lift a weight. The horses tried to draw the load. [These phrases give the true sense.]
try	TRY	v.t.	To examine; to make experiment on; to prove by experiment. Come, try upon yourselves what you have seen me.	To experience; to have knowledge by experience of. Or try the Libyan heat, or Scythian cold.	To prove by a test; as, to try weights and measures by a standard; to try one's opinions by the divine oracles.	To act upon as a test. The fire sev'n times tried this.	To examine judicially by witnesses and the principles of law; as causes tried in court.	To essay; to attempt. Let us try advent'rous work.	To purify; to refine; as silver seven times tried.	To search carefully into. Psalms 11:4.	To use as means; as, to try remedies for a disease.	To strain; as, to try the eyes; the literal sense of the word. To try tallow, etc. is to melt and separate it from the membranes. To tryout, to pursue efforts till a decision is obtained.
try-sail	TRY-SAIL	n.	A sail used by a ship in a storm; literally the strain-sail.
trying	TRYING	ppr.	Examining by searching or comparison with a test; proving; using; straining, etc.	adjective Adapted to try, or put to severe trial.
